RESEARCH

From passive data to useful clinical intelligence.

My research combines longitudinal mobile sensing, ecological momentary assessment, and machine learning to understand changes in behavior and health at the individual level.

01

Digital biomarkers & mental health

Developing robust measures from smartphone and wearable sensors to characterize stress, depression, anxiety, suicidal ideation, and behavioral change outside the clinic.

Passive sensing EMA Digital phenotyping
02

Personalized machine learning

Studying when person-specific models outperform pooled approaches and how models can adapt under sparse, heterogeneous longitudinal data.

SSLTime seriesAdaptive sensing
03

Real-world digital health systems

Building end-to-end sensing workflows and research tools that connect data collection, quality assessment, modeling, and clinical interpretation.

BACKGROUND

Computer science, digital health, and human-centered AI.

I earned my PhD in Computer Science from the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa, where my dissertation centered on personalized machine learning for digital health. Before that, I completed an MSc in Information and Communication Technology — Smart Systems at the University of Turku in Finland.

My current work in Boston focuses on developing methods and infrastructure for clinically grounded digital biomarkers and adaptive mental health technologies.
